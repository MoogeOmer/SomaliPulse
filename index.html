The Art of Hospitality: Why "Soo Dhawoow" Means Everything

In Somali culture, hospitality isn't just being polite—it's a core value. Discover the deep roots behind nomadic greeting traditions...

Culture Desk Oct 9, 2026
In Somali culture, hospitality isn't just being polite—it's an essential cornerstone of our identity. Growing up in the diaspora, hearing "Soo dhawoow" (Welcome) at a family gathering instantly shifts the room, grounding us in values passed down through generations of nomadic life. Back in the rural heartlands, hospitality was a matter of survival and absolute honor; travelers were welcomed into tents, fed before questions were asked, and protected like family. Today, bringing that same warmth into our modern apartments and community centers abroad is how we preserve our roots. Whether it’s preparing a fresh pot of Shaah (spiced tea) or welcoming a newcomer to the neighborhood, these small acts keep our heritage breathing vibrantly across borders.

Word of the Week: Dhaqan & Responsiveness

Breaking down what 'Dhaqan' means for second-generation youth, plus 3 quick Somali phrases you can use with your family...

Language Desk Oct 8, 2026
Welcome back to our weekly language column! This week, we are looking closely at the word **Dhaqan**. While commonly translated simply as "culture," Dhaqan encompasses much more: it covers custom, ethics, behavior, and social etiquette all at once. For youth growing up outside Somalia, maintaining "Dhaqan" often means finding a balance between local modern life and the timeless values taught at home. **3 Quick Phrases to Try This Weekend:** 1. *Ma nabad baa?* (Are you safe / How are you?) 2. *Waan ku mahadsanahay* (Thank you very much) 3. *Gacmo furan baan kugu soo dhaweynayaa* (I welcome you with open arms) Try using these in your next family phone call or community meetup!

Balancing Two Worlds: Growing Up Somali Abroad

How young Somalis navigate school, life in foreign cities, and staying deeply connected to their identity without losing their footing.

Community Member Oct 6, 2026
Growing up between two distinct cultures is a unique journey familiar to thousands of young Somalis around the globe. You walk out the door into a fast-paced Western or international city, navigate school or work, and then return home to a space filled with Somali poetry, food, and traditional values. Many young adults describe this as living with a "dual lens." At times it can feel challenging to figure out where you fit, but over time, it becomes a superpower. You gain a broader worldview, a deep appreciation for community resilience, and a rich linguistic identity. Remember: you don't have to choose between being modern and being Somali. You can successfully be both.
